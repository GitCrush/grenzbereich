# Changelog

The simulation was developed iteratively; each step below was measured against the test harness before it was kept.

## 1.8.0

The autopilot rebuilt on the car's own physics: a line controller that steers from the model, a drift controller for rear-wheel drive computed from the engine's laws, the acceptance bar measured as the distance to the road's edge. Also in this release: rear-wheel drive has a chassis of its own (1150 kg, 50:50, after the front-engined rear-driven rally and club cars); the review of the vehicle model is applied – wet tarmac loses grip with speed, a puddle on loose ground is mud rather than hydroplaning, sand's sinkage acts along the wheel only, braking on a grade gets the right wheel loads, the road height is continuous between track points; and the HUD shows the grip the road itself gives (*Road grip*). Details per build below (newest first).

- **The line controller – the autopilot's steering from the car model (build 2026-10-12b).** The line law built from the physical model instead of pure pursuit.
  - *Why:* the autopilot's line law (`lineSteer`) aims at a point 0.36 s × v + 4 m ahead. Feed-forward: the car's steady state on the line's curvature at the present speed (`lcRef`: lateral velocity, steering, the hands' target; the wheels rolling free). Gas and brake stay with the plan as before. The phantom keeps the line law.

- **The drift controller made robust and safe – still off by default (build 2026-10-10b).** Goal: the 100 % drift share, consistent and safe. Build 10a held slides on a ring and the oval but left the circuit for 2–60 s per lap; the same ring held in one run and was lost in the next. Measured with three new tools – `test/dc-entry.js` (each controller corner: engaged, built, held, given up, off), `test/dc/bench.sh` / `bench3.sh` (ovals R 40–120 m and the circuit over three slide tables measured with other excitation sequences) and `test/dc/robust.js` + `robust.py` (every grid point identified repeatedly, every gain closed on every model).
  - *Root cause – the regulator was not robust to its own measurement:* identified four times, the gain of one run was unstable on the model of another run of the same point (largest closed-loop eigenvalue up to 1.005 per frame; the table's R 55 m model 1.05 on four re-runs). The slowest modes were the path's: the course error's row was fitted from tiny increments (23–40 % explained). Now **the path rows come from geometry** (e′ = −v sin h, h′ = r + β′ − v/R, linearised, β′ from the model's own slip row), and **the weights were searched for the smallest worst case** over all re-runs (β 3, e 10, h 10, inputs 3; before 1/1/1/10): worst 1.0034 → 0.9982, all points stable. The integrals are kept as states but unweighted (with weight they were worse at every setting tried).
  - *The models:* each point identified twice, on the swept band and 3.5 m beside it in the loose gravel, and the slide rows averaged (the band's gain had lightly damped 1.5–1.7 Hz modes and at R 55 m an unstable one on the loose model; on the average the worst case over both was 0.9992); filed under the slip the slide actually settled at (18–24° for a 22° target, and the AWD's steering falls by 1.5–2° per degree there); validated – held 6 s more by the game's own regulator – and planned only if that held; the identification's holder keeps 10a's gentle weights (with the robust ones the RWD table kept 3 of 24 points); the steering weight raised where a 3° slip error would ask for more lock than the slide leaves (RWD countersteers 15–24°).

## 1.0.0 — first public release

**Physics**
- Roll stiffness split into fixed wheel rates and anti-roll bars derived from a roll-gradient setting; geometric load transfer per axle from roll-centre heights.
- Damper curves with knee and bump/rebound asymmetry; progressive bump stops and hard droop stops per wheel; inelastic safety clamps.
- Static toe and bump steer (roll steer follows); camber coefficient 0.25 and camber-dependent peak grip.
- Plated axle differentials with separate drive and coast lock; rigid centre coupling as a momentum-conserving constraint, plated below 100 %; handbrake disconnects the rear axle with a 0.3 s re-engagement.
- Sequential gearbox: ignition-cut upshifts with the clutch engaged, blip downshifts; the automatic no longer hunts while the wheels spin.
- Boost as a spool state that cannot produce torque with the throttle closed.
- Tyre: separate longitudinal and lateral relaxation lengths; force direction blended towards the sliding direction beyond the peak; wedge/plough term with Bekker share and saturating dynamic share.
- Road-fixed reference frame with grade, cross slope and vertical curvature; elevation profile for the circuit.
- Road state: swept band around the driven line, loose material and berm, ruts with wall forces.

**Input**
- Setup per drivetrain: selecting RWD loads its own rear-axle values (diff lock, coast lock, roll distribution, rear toe, roll steer).
- Automatic gearbox as a driver model: waits up to half a second for the corner exit when the driven axle is loaded, then shifts with a throttle ease so the shift kick does not spin the driven wheels; at the limit it holds the gear.

**Driver models for input devices**
- Clutch driver model with launch rpm and stall protection.
- Keyboard threshold braking.
- Phantom plans with the local grip and meters traction.

**Instrumentation**
- Consistency matrix (106 scenarios) and reference benchmark in `test/`.
- Incident detector catches spins with both axles saturated; axle usage is load-weighted.
- On-screen diagnosis when the car cannot move (handbrake, brake, clutch, wheels off the ground).

**Rendering and sound**
- Driver view with elevation: far-to-near road pass, terrain silhouettes, camera following the road plane; top view with relief shading and elevation profile strip.
- Gear whine, anti-lag bangs, limiter cut, shift cut, stone hits, bump-stop thumps.
